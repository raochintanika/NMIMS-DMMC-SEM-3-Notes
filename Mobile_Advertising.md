## 2. Mobile Advertising — 9 Formats

**2.1 Banner Ads** — small rectangular ads (image + copy + CTA e.g. "Buy Now"). Strong for visibility/traffic, average for interaction.

**2.2 Interstitial Ads** — full-screen takeovers. High visibility, lower interaction (users can skip/close). Best for launches/major campaigns.

**2.3 Video Ads** — for awareness/storytelling; communicates more than a static banner.

Types: In-stream, Out-stream, In-feed, Video banner, Social video, Interactive video, 360°.

**In-Stream vs Out-Stream:**

| In-Stream | Out-Stream |
|---|---|
| Plays inside existing video (YouTube pre-roll, OTT) | Plays outside a video player (mid-article) |
| User already watching video | User browsing/reading |
| Stronger video-viewing intent | Extends video reach |

**Video placement:** Pre-Roll (before) → Mid-Roll (during) → Post-Roll (after — limited use, since many users don't finish the video).

**2.4 Playable Ads** — interactive, mostly mobile gaming; lets users try a demo before downloading. High engagement + app acquisition.

**2.5 Rewarded Ads** — user gets a reward for completing an action (e.g. Google Pay rewards, scratch cards). Drives active participation.

**2.6 Notification-Led Ads** — promotional messages via the app's notification system (promos, retargeting, reminders — e.g. a lunch-time offer ping).

**2.7 Rich Media Ads** — interactive/multi-element formats (video + interactivity + 360°). Example: IKEA's AR furniture-visualization experience **(real, verified example — IKEA Place)**.

**2.8 In-App Ads** — describes **placement**, not creative format; an in-app ad can be a banner, video, native, rewarded, or rich-media unit. "In-app" and "banner/video" aren't competing categories.

**2.9 Cross-Promotional Ads** — one app/product in an ecosystem promotes another (e.g. Nykaa Beauty → Nykaa Fashion). Works best with overlapping audiences.

## 3. Consumer Attention & the 6-Second Challenge

Consumers face constant distraction (social, news, apps, notifications, email, video, ads) — marketers must capture attention fast.

**6-second video:** can't do what a 30-second video does — objective is simply **curiosity + attention** (show brand, introduce product, prompt "learn more").

## 4. Consumer Intent

Same ad performs differently by product category, mindset, platform, situation, intent level.

Example: an irrelevant ad interrupting someone's YouTube learning session feels irritating.

**Lesson:** know your audience — otherwise the message becomes generic.

## 5. Brand Integration

Brands embed into entertainment content (movies, web series, YouTube) — works because the brand is remembered **within** content the consumer already cares about. Relevance to audience/content is key.

## 6. In-App Advertising Strategy

Combine formats (banner, video, native, rewarded, rich media) based on objective — **Visibility + Engagement**.

Example: subtle branded elements inside a food-delivery order-tracking screen — visibility without feeling like a traditional ad.

## 7. Cross-Promotion Strategy

Use one product's existing user base to grow another within the same ecosystem (push, SMS, email, loyalty programme, exclusive benefits, in-app promotion).

## 8. Class Activity — Scenario Recommendations

## 9. Key Takeaways

1. Multiple formats, each with a different role
2. Format should match the objective
3. Video = awareness/storytelling
4. Playable/Rewarded = high engagement
5. Rich Media = interaction + brand experience
6. In-App = placement; banner/video/native = creative format
7. Cross-promotion grows other products in the same ecosystem
8. Relevant ads > generic interruptions (intent matters)
9. Short-form ads must grab attention fast
10. Know the audience before deciding creative/format/message

## 1. Where, What & Who of Mobile Advertising

The lecture focused on three basic questions:

- **Where** — Ad placement
- **What** — Ad type and ad format
- **Who** — Target audience

### Placement = Context

The placement of an ad should make sense in the context in which the consumer sees it.

If the placement is random or not contextual, the ad may be present but may not connect with the audience.

**Example:** The same consumer may:

- Read news in the morning
- Think about food around lunch
- Use gaming/OTT apps for entertainment later
- Browse shopping apps at night

The consumer is the same, but the **mindset and environment keep changing**.

> **Main idea:** Placement should connect with the content, context and consumer mindset.

## 2. Mobile Touchpoints & Context

Consumers move across multiple mobile touchpoints during the day.

Examples discussed in class:

- News apps
- Food-delivery apps
- Gaming apps
- OTT platforms
- Shopping apps
- Travel apps
- Calendar/utility apps

### Xiaomi Calendar Example

The professor discussed how Xiaomi had previously used the phone calendar as an advertising touchpoint, including reminders around events, holidays and launches.

The example showed that even a utility such as a calendar can become an advertising placement when the communication is relevant to the user's context.

**Key lesson:**

Relevance + Context → Higher chance of engagement

An ad that is unrelated to the user's situation can feel intrusive, while a relevant ad may be accepted more easily.

## 4. Context & Consumer Mindset

A consumer's mindset changes according to the environment and situation.

For example:

- Morning → information/focused activities
- Afternoon → food/lunch decisions
- Later in the day → entertainment
- Night → shopping/product discovery

Therefore, marketers need to consider **where the consumer is, what they are doing and what mindset they are in** before choosing the placement, format and message.

## 5. Choosing the Right Ad Format

The professor emphasized that **all formats can work, but there should be a logic behind the format selected**.

Consider:

- Objective
- Audience
- Placement
- Context
- Consumer mindset
- Message
- Timing
- Budget

### Example — Duolingo Subscription

For promoting an upgrade from the free version to a paid/subscription feature:

- **Playable** → give users a sneak peek of additional features
- **Video** → explain the product/value visually
- **Banner** → create awareness about the offer
- **Notification-Led** → reach existing app users directly
- **Rewarded Video** → offer an incentive such as temporary access to premium features
- **Interstitial** → more suitable for a major one-day offer than a generic month-long discount because it is a high-impact placement

The key is not to use every format simply because it is available. The choice should have a **clear reason**.

## 6. Interstitials & Campaign Economics

The professor discussed interstitial/full-screen advertising as a **high-impact and relatively expensive** option.

These placements can make more sense for:

- One-day offers
- Major launches
- Important time-sensitive communication

### Class Example

A one-day birthday offer with a large discount can justify a high-impact placement.

A generic discount running for an entire month may not require the same high-impact investment.

> **Marketing lesson:** Match the scale and cost of the format with the campaign objective and duration.

## 7. Mobile Creative Considerations

### Micro Screen

Mobile advertising needs to be designed for the **small screen**.

The creative must communicate clearly even when the consumer is viewing it on a mobile device.

### Clear Value Proposition

The main value proposition should be communicated quickly because users scroll, tap and switch between apps rapidly.

### Movement

Mobile users often multitask and move quickly between screens, so advertising needs to capture attention quickly.

### Privacy & Consent

Privacy is an important consideration in mobile advertising.

SMS marketing was discussed as an example of communication where the consumer may have provided their number to a brand while making a purchase or sharing their details.

**Key idea:** Relevant, consent-based communication is more acceptable than unexpected or irrelevant targeting.

## 8. Targeting — Who Are We Reaching?

Mobile advertising allows marketers to target audiences using different signals.

### Demographic Targeting

Based on factors such as:

- Age
- Gender
- Income
- Occupation

### Geographic Targeting

Based on location such as:

- City
- State
- PIN code
- Radius

**Example:** Searching for **"pizza near me"** shows nearby options rather than pizza places in a distant location.

### Behavioural Targeting

Based on behaviours such as:

- App usage
- Browsing behaviour
- Purchase behaviour
- Purchase intent

### Contextual Targeting

Based on the content or environment the consumer is currently viewing.

**Example:** A travel blog showing an SOTC advertisement is contextually relevant.

### Interest-Based Targeting

Based on interests such as:

- Sports
- Fashion
- Travel
- Gaming

### Device-Based Targeting

Based on device-related characteristics such as:

- Device type
- Operating system
- Connectivity
- Other device characteristics

Some advertising platforms may also provide device price-range or similar device-level targeting signals.

> **Important:** Device characteristics can be used as targeting signals, but they should not automatically be treated as a precise measure of a person's income.

## 9. Ad Relevance

The same ad can perform differently depending on:

- Product category
- Consumer mindset
- Platform
- Situation
- Intent level
- Context

**Example:** An irrelevant ad interrupting someone while they are watching educational content may feel irritating.

The lesson is to understand the audience before deciding the **placement, format, creative and message**.

## 11. Class Activity — Uber: Airport Rides

### Objective

Increase Mumbai airport rides during the festive travel season and increase bookings through Mumbai airport.

### Required Targeting/Planning Areas

- Geographic targeting
- Time targeting
- Behavioural/intent signals
- Mobile placement
- Relevant creative

### Uber Festive Season Strategy

#### 1. Geographic Targeting

- Target Mumbai areas with high airport traffic.
- Focus on airport catchment areas and festive home-to-travel hubs.
- Position airport rides as part of the overall festive journey.

#### 2. Time Targeting

- Increase advertising before major festive travel dates.
- Send booking reminders closer to travel dates.

#### 3. Behaviour / Intent

Target people showing travel intent through:

- Flight searches
- Hotel searches
- Holiday searches

Retarget users showing festive travel interest and reach frequent Uber users where relevant.

#### 4. Messaging & In-App

Use:

- SMS for booking reminders and festive offers
- Email for festive travel offers
- In-app banners
- Push notifications / Notification-Led messages
- Airport ride prompts
- Relevant travel-booking touchpoints where appropriate

#### 5. Festive Offers

Possible offers:

- Festive promo codes for airport rides
- Advance-booking offers
- Benefits for repeat/frequent airport travellers

#### 6. Festive Creative

Use festive visuals combined with travel/upcoming-trip moments.

**Example messages:**

- “Going home this festive season? Ride sorted.”
- “Flight at 6 AM? Book your festive airport ride in advance.”

> **Core idea:** Use the right audience, location, time, placement and creative to reach consumers close to the moment when the airport ride is needed.
