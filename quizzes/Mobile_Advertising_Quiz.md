# Mobile Advertising — Quiz

### Lecture 1 (19 August 2026)

Q1. According to the lecture, what does mobile marketing use to communicate with and influence consumers?

A. Only mobile apps
B. Mobile devices, apps, and networks through specific touchpoints
C. Only SMS messaging
D. Traditional broadcast channels adapted for mobile

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Mobile marketing is defined as the use of mobile devices, apps, and networks through specific touchpoints to communicate with and influence consumers.

Q2. Which of the following is listed as one of the six types of mobile marketing discussed in class?

A. Radio Advertising
B. Location-Based Marketing
C. Print Advertising
D. Outdoor Hoardings

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The six types listed are Mobile Ads, Mobile Messaging, Push Notifications, Mobile App Marketing, Location-Based Marketing, and Mobile Commerce.

Q3. What four factors were identified as making mobile marketing effective?

A. Reach + Frequency + Impressions + Budget
B. Relevance + Timing + Value + Consent
C. Creative + Copy + Design + Placement
D. Awareness + Interest + Desire + Action

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The lecture states effectiveness comes from Relevance + Timing + Value + Consent.

Q4. According to the "FOR" debate point discussed in class, what advantage does mobile marketing offer?

A. Lower personalization than other channels
B. Ability to measure the complete cycle and reach audiences instantly
C. No need for targeting
D. It works exclusively for app-based businesses

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The "FOR" mobile marketing debate point states it can be more personalized, better at targeting, reach audiences instantly, and measure the complete cycle.

Q5. In the Objectives of Mobile Marketing sequence, what comes immediately after "Lead Generation"?

A. Awareness
B. App Acquisition
C. Retention
D. Loyalty

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The objectives sequence is Awareness → Lead Generation → App Acquisition → Sales & Conversion → Engagement → Retention → Loyalty.

### Lecture 2 (26 August 2026)

Q1. In the FreshBite case study, what is the "Mobile Moment" concept described as?

A. A discount applied only on mobile devices
B. Targeting the exact moment a consumer needs the product, e.g. a lunchtime ping
C. The moment a user installs the app
D. A one-time onboarding notification

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The "Mobile Moment" refers to reaching the consumer at the precise moment of need, such as a 12 PM lunch ping or dinner-hour reminder — relevance at the point of need.

Q2. Which ad format is described as allowing users to try a demo before downloading an app?

A. Interstitial Ads
B. Playable Ads
C. Banner Ads
D. Rich Media Ads

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Playable Ads are interactive, mostly used in mobile gaming, letting users try a demo before downloading — associated with high engagement and app acquisition.

Q3. According to the lecture, what is the key distinction between In-Stream and Out-Stream video ads?

A. In-Stream plays inside an existing video; Out-Stream plays outside a video player (e.g., mid-article)
B. In-Stream is only for mobile games; Out-Stream is only for websites
C. They are identical concepts with different names
D. Out-Stream requires stronger existing viewing intent than In-Stream

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** In-Stream plays inside existing video content (e.g., YouTube pre-roll) where the user is already watching video, while Out-Stream plays outside a video player, extending video reach to users who are browsing/reading.

Q4. Why is "In-App Advertising" described as different from formats like banner or video ads?

A. It cannot use banners or videos at all
B. It describes a placement, not a creative format — a banner, video, native, rewarded, or rich-media unit can all be delivered in-app
C. It is only usable for cross-promotion
D. It is not measurable

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The lecture clarifies that "In-App" describes placement, not creative format; an in-app ad can take the form of any other format, such as banner, video, native, rewarded, or rich-media.

Q5. In the class scenario recommendations, which formats were recommended for a new smartphone launch, and why?

A. Notification-Led and Banner, because they are cheapest
B. Video, Rich Media, and Interstitial, because the launch needs awareness, demonstration, and high visibility
C. Playable only, because gaming audiences are the target
D. Cross-Promotional Ads only

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A new smartphone launch was matched with Video, Rich Media and Interstitial formats to deliver awareness, product demonstration, and high visibility.

Q6. What is the main objective of the "6-second challenge" described in the lecture?

A. To fully explain all product features within 6 seconds
B. To capture curiosity and attention quickly, given constant consumer distraction
C. To replace all 30-second video ads
D. To increase CPM rates

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A 6-second video can't do what a 30-second video does; its objective is simply curiosity and attention — introducing the brand/product and prompting "learn more."

### Lecture 3 (16 September 2026)

Q1. According to the lecture, what do the three core questions "Where, What, and Who" refer to in mobile advertising?

A. Where = Budget, What = Audience, Who = Timing
B. Where = Ad placement, What = Ad type and format, Who = Target audience
C. Where = Creative, What = Platform, Who = Objective
D. Where = Timing, What = Budget, Who = Placement

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The lecture frames Where as ad placement, What as ad type and ad format, and Who as the target audience.

Q2. What is the key distinction the lecture draws between "Ad Type" and "Ad Format"?

A. They mean exactly the same thing
B. Ad Type is the communication objective/experience; Ad Format is the creative shape, size, style, and delivery
C. Ad Type refers only to video; Ad Format refers only to banners
D. Ad Format determines the target audience; Ad Type determines the budget

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Ad Type refers to the communication objective or advertising experience, while Ad Format refers to the creative shape, size, style, and way the ad is delivered.

Q3. In the Xiaomi calendar example, what lesson does the lecture draw?

A. Calendars should never be used for advertising
B. Even a utility app like a calendar can become an effective ad placement if the communication is relevant to the user's context
C. Calendar ads always perform worse than banner ads
D. Xiaomi discontinued using the calendar for ads due to poor performance

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The example shows that relevance and context can make even a utility touchpoint like a calendar an effective advertising placement.

Q4. For the Duolingo subscription-upgrade example discussed in class, which format was recommended to "give users a sneak peek of additional features"?

A. Banner
B. Playable
C. Notification-Led
D. Interstitial

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Playable format was recommended to give users a sneak peek of premium features before committing to a subscription upgrade.

Q5. Why does the lecture describe Interstitial ads as more suitable for a one-day offer than a month-long generic discount?

A. Interstitials are cheaper for long campaigns
B. Interstitials are high-impact and relatively expensive, better matched to time-sensitive, major promotions
C. Interstitials cannot be used for discounts
D. Interstitials always have low visibility

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Interstitial/full-screen ads are high-impact and relatively expensive, making them more suitable for one-day offers or major launches than for a generic long-running discount.

Q6. Which targeting type is illustrated by the example of a travel blog showing a travel-agency (SOTC) advertisement?

A. Demographic Targeting
B. Geographic Targeting
C. Contextual Targeting
D. Device-Based Targeting

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Contextual targeting is based on the content or environment the consumer is currently viewing — a travel-related ad on a travel blog is contextually relevant.
