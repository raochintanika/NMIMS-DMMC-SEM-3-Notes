### Lecture 1 (17 August 2026)

Q1. According to the lecture, media is described as the "4th pillar of democracy" alongside which institutions?

A. Legislature, Executive, Judiciary
B. Government, Corporations, NGOs
C. Legislature, Executive, Media Houses
D. Judiciary, Police, Executive

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** The lecture describes media as the 4th pillar of democracy, alongside the legislature, executive, and judiciary.

Q2. What is an "Echo Chamber," as defined in class?

A. A method of fact-checking news content
B. A bubble where a person's opinions are reinforced based on the data/content they keep receiving
C. A legal defence against defamation
D. A type of algorithmic bias correction tool

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** An Echo Chamber is a bubble where a person's opinions are reinforced based on the data/content they keep receiving, usually via algorithmic recommendation.

Q3. According to the lecture's distinction, what differentiates "Evolution" from "Appropriation" in content/culture?

A. Evolution and Appropriation are identical concepts
B. Evolution is organic change within the same context/culture; Appropriation is borrowing from another culture/context, often without credit
C. Appropriation always happens within the same community
D. Evolution only applies to legal contexts

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Evolution refers to content/meaning changing naturally within the same context, while Appropriation refers to taking content/meaning and using it in a different context, commonly without credit.

Q4. What is an "Infodemic," as discussed in class?

A. A pandemic caused by social media
B. An overload of information — accurate or not — that makes it hard to find trustworthy sources
C. A legal term for defamation involving multiple parties
D. A form of algorithmic bias

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** An Infodemic is an overload of information (accurate or not) that makes it hard for people to find trustworthy sources; the term originated in public health but is now used more broadly.

Q5. According to the formula discussed for endorsements, how is "Economic Associative Value" calculated?

A. Recognition Value − Commercial Value
B. Recognition Value + Commercial Value
C. Commercial Value ÷ Recognition Value
D. Recognition Value × Commercial Value

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The lecture presents the formula: Recognition Value + Commercial Value = Economic Associative Value.

Q6. What is "Algorithmic Bias," according to the lecture?

A. A bias intentionally added by content creators
B. Bias built into a program/platform because it was trained on unbalanced or unfair data
C. A legal defence in defamation cases
D. A form of echo chamber specific to news apps

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Algorithmic Bias is defined as bias built into a program or platform because it was trained on unbalanced or unfair data.

### Lecture 2 (24 August 2026)

Q1. What does "Right of Publicity (ROP)" refer to, according to the lecture?

A. The right to publish news without restriction
B. The right related to the commercial use of a celebrity/influencer's identity, including name, image, and likeness
C. A government-issued press credential
D. The right to sue for false statements only

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** ROP covers the commercial use of a celebrity/influencer's identity — including name, image, likeness, voice, persona, and personality rights.

Q2. According to the "Fame ↑ → Privacy ↓" principle discussed in class, what happens to a public figure's Right to Privacy as their fame increases?

A. Their privacy protections increase proportionally
B. Their privacy becomes harder to maintain, especially on matters of public interest
C. Fame has no relationship with privacy
D. Privacy rights are eliminated entirely once someone becomes famous

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** As fame increases, privacy becomes harder to maintain — courts generally hold public figures to a narrower zone of privacy, especially on matters of public interest.

Q3. What is the key difference between Libel and Slander, according to the lecture?

A. Libel is spoken; Slander is written
B. Libel is harm through written/published communication; Slander is harm through spoken communication
C. They are the same, just used in different countries
D. Libel applies only to celebrities; Slander applies only to private individuals

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Libel refers to reputational harm through written or published communication, while Slander refers to harm through spoken communication.

Q4. Which of the following is listed as one of the key elements needed to establish defamation?

A. The statement must be made anonymously
B. The statement must be published to a third party
C. The statement must be made by a public figure
D. The statement must involve financial loss

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The key elements of defamation include a defamatory statement, a false statement, specific reference to an identifiable person, and publication to a third party.

Q5. In the Manisha Koirala v. Shashilal Nair case discussed in class, what was the outcome?

A. The court granted the injunction to stop the release of the scenes
B. The court dismissed her plea for an injunction
C. The case was settled out of court
D. The film was permanently banned

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Bombay High Court dismissed Manisha Koirala's plea for an injunction against the release of the scenes, despite her claims of defamation, invasion of privacy, and breach of contract.

Q6. Which of the following is listed as a defence against defamation in the lecture?

A. Popularity of the speaker
B. Truth
C. Financial compensation to the affected party
D. Anonymity of the source

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The defences against defamation discussed are Truth, Public Interest, Good Faith, and Privileged Communication.
