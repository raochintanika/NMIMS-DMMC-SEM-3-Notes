# Digital Media Planning & Buying — Quiz

### Lecture 1 (20 August 2026)

Q1. What is the primary objective of media planning, according to the lecture?

A. Maximize ROI *after* ad space is purchased
B. Maximize ROI *before* ad space is purchased
C. Minimize creative production costs
D. Guarantee the highest possible impressions regardless of audience

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Media planning is a strategic process focused on maximizing ROI *before* ad space is purchased, by deciding where, when and how ads are shown.

Q2. In the Media Mix hierarchy discussed in class, what comes immediately after "Platform"?

A. Channel
B. Media Vehicle
C. Content Format
D. Campaign Phase

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Media Mix hierarchy discussed is Channel → Platform → Media Vehicle → Content Format.

Q3. An ad generates 80 impressions and reaches 20 unique people. What is the frequency?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 400
D. 0.25

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Frequency = Impressions ÷ Reach = 80 ÷ 20 = 4, matching the class example.

Q4. According to the lecture, which statement best describes the relationship between Earned, Owned and Paid media?

A. Earned media is entirely independent of Owned and Paid media
B. Earned media depends on Owned and Paid media
C. Paid media depends on Earned media
D. Owned media depends on Earned media

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The notes state "Earned depends on Owned + Paid" — media coverage and word-of-mouth build on a brand's own channels and its paid efforts.

Q5. In the O2 "Be More Dog" campaign example, what key principle did the lecture highlight?

A. Focus all budget on a single platform for consistency
B. Diversify across multiple platforms while staying true to the core idea
C. Avoid outdoor/offline activities entirely
D. Use only paid media, with no owned or earned channels

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The campaign used multiple platforms (YouTube, Vine, X, blogs, TV, retail, etc.) while keeping the core idea consistent — illustrating diversification, awareness and shareability.

Q6. In the three Campaign Phases discussed, which phase is described as having "maximum intensity"?

A. Teaser
B. Launch
C. Sustenance
D. Optimization

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Launch phase delivers the main campaign at maximum intensity, while Teaser builds curiosity beforehand and Sustenance maintains attention afterward (and can be extended).

### Lecture 2 (27 August 2026)

Q1. In the communication process model discussed in class, what does "Noise" refer to?

A. The volume of paid media spend
B. Anything that interferes with the intended communication at any stage
C. The frequency of ad exposure
D. A KPI used to measure feedback

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Noise is anything that distorts intended communication — such as language/cultural barriers, tech issues, or competition — and can occur at any stage between Sender and Receiver.

Q2. What is the key learning highlighted by the Coca-Cola Holiday Campaign example?

A. Positive intended messages always generate positive reactions
B. A campaign can receive negative reactions even with a positive intended message
C. Feedback is not important once a campaign launches
D. Noise only affects traditional media, not digital campaigns

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The lecture noted that "what we intended to communicate" is not always "what the audience actually understood," so even a positive intended message can generate negative reactions.

Q3. What does CRO (Conversion Rate Optimization) aim to achieve, according to the lecture?

A. Increasing traffic by increasing ad spend
B. Getting a higher ROI/conversion rate from existing traffic, without increasing spend
C. Reducing frequency to avoid overexposure
D. Improving brand recall regardless of conversions

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** CRO is defined as improving the ROI/conversion rate from *existing* traffic without additional spend — same budget, better conversion performance.

Q4. Which of the following is listed as a "Trust Builder" CRO lever?

A. Retargeting
B. Cart Abandonment Optimization
C. Reviews and testimonials
D. Personalised Marketing

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Trust Builders include reviews, ratings, testimonials, unboxing/influencer videos, founder presence, and similar elements that build customer confidence.

Q5. According to the lecture, which of these is a typical column in a Media Plan document?

A. Frequency Cap Duration
B. Budget
C. Noise Type
D. Encoding Format

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Typical Media Plan columns discussed are Platform, Audience, Objective, Budget, Time & Schedule, and KPIs.

Q6. A marketer notices many customers add items to their cart but don't complete the purchase. Which CRO lever specifically addresses this scenario?

A. Creative Optimization
B. Cart Abandonment Optimization
C. Landing Page Optimization
D. Trust Builders

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Cart Abandonment Optimization directly targets users who added items to cart but did not purchase, using retargeting, discounts, and urgency messaging.

### Lecture 3 (3 September 2026)

Q1. In the funnel discussed in class (Awareness → Findability → Reputation → Conversion → Advocacy), which metric belongs to the "Findability" stage?

A. Impressions
B. Clicks
C. Sales
D. Follower growth

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Findability is measured via Clicks, Landing Page Views, Video Views and Engagement, while Impressions belongs to Awareness and Sales to Conversion.

Q2. Which reputation-measurement tool discussed in class is used for social listening/mention monitoring?

A. Modash
B. Brand24
C. Comscore
D. Statista

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Brand24 was described as a tool for social listening and mention monitoring, while Modash is used for influencer/follower analysis.

Q3. A campaign generates 150 impressions and reaches 50 unique people. What is the frequency?

A. 2
B. 3
C. 5
D. 7,500

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Frequency = Impressions ÷ Reach = 150 ÷ 50 = 3, matching the class example.

Q4. According to the lecture, when is a YouTube skippable in-stream ad counted as a "view"?

A. As soon as the ad starts playing
B. After 30 seconds are watched (or the full ad, if shorter), or on interaction
C. Only if the viewer clicks the CTA
D. Non-skippable ads always count as views

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** A skippable in-stream ad view is counted at 30 seconds watched (or the full ad if shorter) or on interaction with the CTA/card/banner; non-skippable ads don't generate a "view" metric at all.

Q5. What principle did the lecture emphasize regarding marketing funnel models such as AIDA, 5A, and See-Think-Do-Care?

A. Only one specific model should ever be used
B. Advocacy should always be included, whichever model is chosen
C. Awareness is not necessary if Conversion metrics are strong
D. These models apply only to traditional media

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The lecture stressed: "Whichever model you use, always include Advocacy."

Q6. What is the correct formula for CPM (Cost Per Mille) discussed in class?

A. Spend ÷ Clicks
B. Spend ÷ Impressions
C. (Spend ÷ Impressions) × 1,000
D. (Spend ÷ Clicks) × 1,000

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** CPM = (Spend ÷ Impressions) × 1,000, giving cost per 1,000 impressions — e.g., ₹1,000 spend ÷ 50,000 impressions × 1,000 = ₹20.

### Lecture 4 (10 September 2026)

Q1. According to the lecture, how should a marketer remarket to a user who reached the product page but did not proceed?

A. Show a generic brand-awareness ad
B. Remarket with an offer/discount
C. Stop targeting the user entirely
D. Send a loyalty reward immediately

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** For users who reached the product page without action, the lecture recommends remarketing with an offer/discount — distinct from the homepage or cart-stage remarketing actions.

Q2. What is the correct formula for CTR (Click-Through Rate) as discussed in class?

A. (Conversions ÷ Clicks) × 100
B. (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100
C. Spend ÷ Clicks
D. (Impressions ÷ Clicks) × 100

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** CTR = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100. Being a ratio, it allows fair comparison between campaigns regardless of scale.

Q3. Ad A has 5,00,000 impressions and 1% CTR; Ad B has 1,20,000 impressions and 2% CTR. According to the lecture's reasoning, which ad "wins" on CTR?

A. Ad A, because it has more impressions
B. Ad B, despite having fewer impressions
C. Both perform identically
D. Neither — CTR cannot be compared across campaigns

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** This example shows that raw impression/click counts can mislead — CTR, being a ratio, shows Ad B performs better despite fewer impressions.

Q4. What does CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) measure, according to the lecture's formula?

A. Marketing Spend ÷ New Customers Acquired only
B. (Marketing Spend + Sales Spend) ÷ New Customers Acquired
C. Revenue from Ads ÷ Ad Spend
D. Average Order Value × Purchase Frequency

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** CAC = (Marketing Spend + Sales Spend) ÷ New Customers Acquired. The lecture emphasized including sales-side costs (CRM, sales team, etc.), not just ad spend.

Q5. In the class ROI example (₹75,000 spend, 250 new customers, ₹1,000 avg. purchase value, ₹600 fulfilment cost/order), what was the calculated ROI?

A. 25%
B. 33.3%
C. 50%
D. 100%

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Net profit before ad spend = ₹1,00,000; ROI = (1,00,000 − 75,000) ÷ 75,000 × 100 = 33.3%.

Q6. What is the key distinction between ROAS and ROI, as discussed in the lecture?

A. ROAS focuses on profit; ROI focuses on revenue
B. ROAS focuses on revenue; ROI accounts for cost/net profit
C. They are calculated identically
D. ROAS applies only to offline media

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** ROAS = Revenue from Ads ÷ Ad Spend, focusing on revenue, while ROI = ((Gain − Cost) ÷ Cost) × 100, factoring in net gain after cost.
