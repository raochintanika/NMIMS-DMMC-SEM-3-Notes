# OTT Advertising — Quiz

### Lecture 1 (21 August 2026)

Q1. According to the lecture, what does OTT stand for and what does it mean?

A. Online Television Technology — cable-based delivery
B. Over The Top — content/services delivered over the internet, bypassing traditional broadcast/cable infrastructure
C. Optimal Traffic Targeting — an ad-buying method
D. Over The Television — a satellite delivery system

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** OTT = Over The Top, referring to content/services delivered over the internet, bypassing traditional broadcast/cable infrastructure; Netflix was used as the class example.

Q2. How is TRP (Television Rating Point) measured, according to the lecture?

A. By counting every household in the country
B. Based on sampling, not a full population count
C. Only through online surveys
D. By tracking OTT platform logins

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** TRP is based on sampling rather than a full population count — in India it is measured via a panel of households, extrapolated to represent the whole population.

Q3. In the lecture, "Hoarding" was discussed in two different contexts. What were they?

A. Outdoor billboard advertising and TV rating measurement
B. IP tracking/approximation for audience exposure, and Netflix's "Hoarding" PR campaign
C. Content licensing and subscription pricing
D. Frequency capping and budget scheduling

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The two uses of "Hoarding" discussed were Hoarding Data (IP tracking/approximation for audience exposure) and the Netflix "Hoarding" Campaign (a PR stunt/brand-positioning example) — the notes flag these as distinct concepts to confirm with the professor.

Q4. Which OTT monetization model relies on a recurring subscription fee, according to the lecture?

A. TVOD
B. AVOD
C. FVOD
D. SVOD

**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** SVOD (Subscription VOD) involves a recurring subscription fee, distinguishing it from TVOD (pay/rent per title), AVOD (ad-funded, free to watch), and FVOD (free content, not necessarily ad-funded).

Q5. According to the Traditional TV vs OTT comparison table, which trait describes OTT rather than Traditional TV?

A. Fixed schedule / EPG
B. Family/group viewing
C. On-demand, individual consumption
D. Limited user-level tracking

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** OTT is described as on-demand with individual consumption, non-linear and globally accessible, contrasting with Traditional TV's fixed schedule, group viewing, and limited tracking.

Q6. What is TVOD, as defined in the lecture?

A. Free content used for sampling/engagement
B. Pay or rent per title, such as renting a new release for a limited window
C. A recurring subscription fee
D. Content monetized entirely through advertisements

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** TVOD (Transactional VOD) means paying or renting per title, such as renting a new release for a limited window, sometimes with early access.

### Lecture 2 (4 September 2026)

Q1. What does FAST stand for, according to the lecture?

A. Fully Automated Streaming Technology
B. Free Ad-Supported Streaming Television
C. Fast Access Subscription Television
D. Flexible Ad Streaming Technology

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** FAST = Free Ad-Supported Streaming Television — linear, scheduled (EPG-style) programming delivered over the internet and funded by ads, requiring no subscription or login.

Q2. In the OTT Ecosystem flow discussed in class, what is the correct order?

A. Internet → Cloud → CDN → Content → User Device
B. Content → Cloud → CDN → Internet → User Device
C. Content → CDN → Cloud → Internet → User Device
D. User Device → CDN → Cloud → Internet → Content

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The OTT Ecosystem flow is Content → Cloud → CDN → Internet → User Device, where Cloud stores/processes content and CDN distributes it efficiently to the device.

Q3. In the OTT case study discussed ("Anfiction," 18M installs), why was a hybrid monetisation model (SVOD + AVOD + FVOD) chosen?

A. Because a single model always maximises revenue
B. To cater to young audiences with varying time-on-platform, occasional viewing, and different willingness to pay
C. Because advertising was banned on the platform
D. Because subscription-only models are illegal for children's content

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The hybrid model was chosen to cater to young audiences, varying time-on-platform, occasional viewers, and different willingness to pay across the user base.

Q4. According to the lecture, what is the key distinction between a "User" and an "Active User"?

A. A User has installed/has an account, but is not necessarily active; an Active User actually engages with the platform
B. They are the same thing
C. A User is always more engaged than an Active User
D. An Active User refers only to paying subscribers

**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A User has installed the app or has an account but is not necessarily active, while an Active User actually engages — watching, clicking, browsing, or interacting.

Q5. Which of the following is listed as an OTT engagement tactic in the lecture?

A. Reducing content variety
B. Personalised recommendations
C. Removing watchlists
D. Eliminating notifications

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Engagement tactics listed include Personalised recommendations, Notifications, Offers, Watchlist, Watch Party, Rewards, Newer content, and Nostalgia value/calendar.

Q6. What does CLV/LTV represent in the context of OTT platforms, according to the lecture?

A. The cost of acquiring a new customer
B. A user's long-term value to the platform, beyond just acquisition
C. The cost of content localisation
D. The frequency of ad impressions per user

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** CLV/LTV (Customer/Lifetime Value) represents the goal of understanding a user's long-term value to the platform, rather than focusing on acquisition alone.
