## 1. OTT
**OTT = Over The Top** — content/services delivered over the internet, bypassing traditional broadcast/cable infrastructure.

- Netflix used as the class example.
- OTT is a **non-linear** medium.
- **DTH** = satellite/signal-based delivery
- **Cable** = physical cable network
- **OTT** = internet-based delivery

## 2. Traditional TV vs OTT

| Traditional TV | OTT |
|---|---|
| Fixed schedule / EPG | On-demand |
| Local or national | Global content |
| Family/group viewing | Individual consumption |
| Limited user-level tracking | Tracks detailed user behaviour/metrics |
| Geographic limitations | No traditional geographic boundary |
| Linear | Non-linear |

## 8. OTT Monetization Models
*(Fact-check: all four definitions below match standard industry usage.)*
- **TVOD** (Transactional VOD): pay/rent per title — e.g., renting a new release for a limited window, sometimes with early access.
- **AVOD** (Advertising VOD): free to watch, monetized via ads.
- **FVOD** (Free VOD): free content, often used for sampling/engagement (distinct from AVOD in that it isn't necessarily ad-funded — though in practice the two overlap heavily).
- **SVOD** (Subscription VOD): recurring subscription fee; some plans may still include ads (an "ad-supported subscription tier").

### Quick Revision
- **OTT:** Internet-delivered, non-linear, on-demand
- **Traditional TV:** Scheduled, linear, mass-audience
- **TRP:** Sample-based TV measurement
- **TVOD / AVOD / FVOD / SVOD:** Pay-per-title / Ad-funded / Free / Subscription

## 1. FAST Channels
**FAST = Free Ad-Supported Streaming Television** — linear, scheduled (EPG-style) programming delivered over the internet, funded by ads.

*(Clarification: the "Free" in FAST is a defining feature, not just implied — FAST channels require no subscription or login at all, unlike SVOD. Your notes' formula "Linear programming + Internet delivery + Ads" is accurate but worth remembering the "no-subscription/free-to-access" part explicitly, since that's what distinguishes FAST from a paid vMVPD service.)*

## 2. OTT Ecosystem
**Content → Cloud → CDN → Internet → User Device**

- **Cloud:** stores/processes content
- **CDN (Content Delivery Network):** distributes streamed content efficiently from cloud to device

## 3. Cloud Infrastructure
Major providers discussed: **Azure, AWS** (both real, major cloud infrastructure providers — correct).

## 4. OTT Case Study
- Genre: Animation/Fiction, "Anfiction" (as named in class), 18M installs
- **Monetisation:** Hybrid — SVOD + AVOD + FVOD
- **Why hybrid:** caters to young audiences, varying time-on-platform, occasional viewers, and different willingness to pay across the user base

## 5. Monetisation Factors
Two major cost drivers: **Cost of the App** + **Cost of Content**

**App costs:** development, technology, maintenance, resources, marketing, cloud infrastructure, CDN
**Content costs:** production, acquisition, rights, localisation, marketing

**Content acquisition example:** Korean content → acquired → localised into Hindi/English for the target market.

## 6. Top Content Categories (as discussed)
Bollywood, Astrology, Spiritual/Devotional, Sports

## 7. OTT Marketing Costs
**Traditional media** (Print, Outdoor, TV, Radio) — challenges: attention, cost, measurability, visibility, limited audience-level data.
**Digital media** (social, email, performance marketing, influencers, telemarketing) — advantage: far more measurable audience behaviour data.

## 8. CDN (recap)
**CDN = Content Delivery Network** — part of the delivery infrastructure: **Cloud → CDN → Internet → Device → Stream**

## 9–10. User Journey: Acquisition → User → Active User
- **Acquisition:** Installs → Users
- **User:** has installed/has an account — not necessarily active
- **Active User:** actually engages (watches, clicks, browses, interacts)

> Install ≠ active use — an installed app doesn't guarantee an engaged user.

## 11. Engagement & CLV/LTV
Key metrics: Installs, Users, Active Users.
**CLV/LTV (Customer/Lifetime Value):** the goal isn't just acquisition — it's understanding a user's long-term value to the platform.

## 12. Subscription Periods
Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly — platforms can structure plans around different billing cadences.

## 13. Engagement Tactics
1. Personalised recommendations
2. Notifications (new episode/content, reminders)
3. Offers (subscription, re-subscription, consumption)
4. Watchlist
5. Watch Party (shared viewing)
6. Rewards
7. Newer content (reason to return)
8. Nostalgia value/calendar (old shows, festivals, anniversaries, seasonal content)

## 14. Overall OTT Flow
**Acquisition → Install → User → Active User → Engagement → Subscription/Revenue → Retention**

> Acquisition alone isn't enough — platforms must convert installs into active, engaged, retained, high-LTV users.

### Quick Revision — 4 September 2026
- **FAST:** Free + Linear/EPG-style + Internet + Ads
- **OTT Ecosystem:** Cloud → CDN → Internet → Device → Streaming
- **Monetisation:** SVOD + AVOD + FVOD = Hybrid Model
- **Costs:** App Cost + Content Cost (Production + Acquisition + Localisation + Rights; Marketing + Cloud + Maintenance + Resources + CDN)
- **Content categories:** Bollywood, Astrology, Spiritual/Devotional, Sports
- **User journey:** Installs → Users → Active Users → Engagement
- **Value metric:** CLV/LTV
- **Subscription periods:** Monthly / Quarterly / Yearly
- **Engagement tactics:** Recommendations, Notifications, Offers, Watchlist, Watch Party, Rewards, New Content, Nostalgia/Calendar

> **Core learning:** Acquiring a user is only the beginning — the platform must keep them active and engaged to generate long-term value.

## 1. OTT Monetization — Who Pays?

The OTT ecosystem always has a cost. Someone in the ecosystem has to bear that cost.

The cost may be borne by:
- Consumer
- Brand
- Platform
- Content provider/developer

The lecture connected this to the monetization models discussed in the previous lectures.

> **Key idea:** OTT content and streaming are not free to produce or operate. The cost is carried by someone in the ecosystem.

## 2. OTT Business Case — CFO Perspective

A case study was discussed from the perspective of a **CFO (Chief Financial Officer)**.

The CFO's role is to manage:
- Finances
- Profitability
- Balance sheet
- P&L

### Example discussed

A hypothetical OTT company has:
- **100 subscribers**
- **₹199 per month** subscription

The subscription revenue represents the **top-line revenue** before reductions/costs.

The lecture then examined the major costs that an OTT company has to manage.

## 3. Major OTT Cost Components

Three primary costs discussed were:

### 1. Technology
Includes:
- Cloud
- Storage
- Product development
- CDN
- Streaming/product infrastructure

### 2. Content
Includes:
- Content creation
- Content acquisition
- Regular content development
- Refreshing the content library

### 3. Marketing
Includes:
- Performance marketing
- Communication
- Influencer marketing
- PR/marketing activities

> Even a small OTT company needs some form of marketing and communication to acquire customers.

Other operating costs can also include:
- Employees/resources
- Operations
- Rent
- Laptops/equipment
- Legal costs
- Research

## 4. Content Development as a Continuous Cost

Content cannot remain static forever.

If an OTT platform keeps showing the same content without refreshing its library:
- Users may stop returning
- Subscribers may churn
- Revenue can decline

Therefore, OTT platforms need to keep investing in **content development and content refreshment**.

This creates a balance between:
- Content investment
- Subscriber growth
- Marketing investment
- Profitability

> **CFO perspective:** The platform has to continuously balance how much it spends on content and marketing against the revenue generated by subscribers.

## 5. OTT Profitability Cycle

The lecture discussed the relationship between content, subscribers and marketing.

One side of the cycle is:

**More Content → More Subscribers**

From the CFO perspective:

**More Investment in Content → Potential Subscriber Growth → Higher Revenue**

But marketing is also required to establish and grow that revenue.

Therefore, OTT profitability involves continuously balancing:
- Content
- Technology
- Marketing
- Operations
- Subscriber growth
- Revenue

## 8. Customer Churn in OTT

**Churn** means a subscriber stops or cancels their subscription.

For an OTT platform:

**Subscriber → Unsubscribes → Customer Churn**

Customer churn directly affects the profitability of customer acquisition.

For example, if a platform spends heavily on marketing to acquire a customer but the customer leaves quickly, the platform may not recover its acquisition cost.

## 9. Major Reasons for OTT Churn

Reasons discussed in class include:

### Content
- Content becomes dated
- Content is not refreshed
- Content does not match the customer's genre/interests

### Price
- Subscription becomes expensive
- Price changes after an introductory offer
- Customer feels the price is not justified

### Too Many Ads
A highly cluttered advertising experience can affect the customer experience.

### Notifications
Customers may dislike excessive notifications and interruptions.

### Technology / App Experience
Issues discussed included:
- App UI
- App performance
- Storage limitations
- App size

### Event-Based Subscription
Some customers subscribe for a particular event or sports season and unsubscribe once the event is over.

Examples discussed included sports and other event-based content.

## 10. Introductory Pricing

OTT platforms may use an introductory price to acquire customers.

Example discussed:

**₹99 introductory price → ₹199 renewal price**

A customer may subscribe because of the initial lower price and later unsubscribe when the price increases.

Therefore, **price changes** can contribute to churn.

## 11. Paid User vs Free User — Case Study

A case study compared two users:

### Free User
- Pays **₹0**
- Watches **40 hours per week**

### Paid User
- Pays **₹199 per month**
- Watches **2 hours per week**

Asked to evaluate:

- Which user is more valuable?
- Why?
- Which user is more likely to be retained?
- What should the platform consider before making the decision?

### Key discussion

For an **ad-supported OTT platform**, the free user's viewing volume can be valuable because advertising revenue depends on impressions and audience volume.

The lecture highlighted that **CPM in India is relatively low**, so ad-supported OTT models need significant audience volume for advertising revenue to make sense.

Therefore:

**Ad-Supported OTT → Volume is important**

For a paid platform:

**Paid User → Direct subscription revenue**

The value of each user depends on the platform's business model and strategy.

> There is no single right answer. The platform has to consider its own business model, pricing, audience, marketing and customer acquisition strategy.

## 12. OTT Pricing & Business Strategy

The lecture discussed how OTT platforms balance:

**Price ↔ Volume ↔ Cost ↔ Revenue**

A platform may choose:
- Lower pricing to acquire more subscribers
- Higher pricing with a smaller/niche audience
- Different pricing for different markets
- Short-duration or "sachet" pricing
- Longer subscription plans

### Sachet Pricing

Examples discussed included:
- 1-day pricing
- 2-day pricing
- 7-day pricing

However, the platform must consider whether short-duration pricing actually produces enough lifetime value to justify acquisition and marketing costs.

> **Key idea:** Pricing decisions must be connected to customer acquisition cost, subscriber volume and expected revenue.

## 17. How Content Reaches an OTT Platform

The lecture discussed several ways an OTT platform can acquire or bring content onto its platform.

### 1. Produce the Content

The platform produces its own content.

### 2. Licence / Buy Content

The platform acquires rights to content created by someone else.

### 3. Live Streaming

The platform can stream live events such as:
- Sports
- Festivals
- Competitions
- Conferences

A licence or arrangement with the content/event owner may be required.

### 4. Commission a Project

The OTT platform can commission a project.

For example:
- A writer/director has a script
- The OTT platform commissions the project
- Someone else produces the content
- The platform receives the agreed rights/content

### 5. Co-Production

Content can also be produced jointly by multiple parties.

## 18. Exclusive vs Non-Exclusive Content

### Exclusive

Content rights are available exclusively to one platform for the agreed scope.

Example:

A movie is available only on one OTT platform.

### Non-Exclusive

The same content can be available on more than one platform.

The platform may choose non-exclusive rights when:
- The budget is limited
- Exclusive rights are too expensive
- It wants to add more content within the available budget

## 19. Language-Specific Rights

An OTT platform may acquire rights for a particular language.

For example:
- Hindi rights
- Punjabi rights
- English rights

A platform may choose only the language rights relevant to its audience instead of acquiring rights for every language.

## 20. Multi-Language Rights

A platform can also acquire rights across multiple languages.

For example, content may be adapted or made available in:
- Hindi
- Punjabi
- English

The decision depends on the platform's audience and business strategy.

## 21. Geo-Fencing & Geographic Rights

Content rights can also be restricted by geography.

Examples discussed:
- India rights
- Worldwide rights
- US and Canada
- Middle East
- Other specific regions/countries

The platform chooses geographic rights based on:
- Where its consumers are
- Budget
- Business strategy
- Expected demand

## 22. Acquiring Existing Content

An OTT platform may acquire existing content when:
- Good content already exists in the market
- The content has an established fan base
- It would be difficult or expensive to recreate
- Subscribers are likely to value the content

Examples discussed included older/classic shows and regional content.

## 23. Localisation & Format Rights

OTT platforms can acquire the **format rights** of an existing show and then localise it.

The original format can be adapted using:
- Local actors
- Local language
- Local production

The lecture discussed examples such as **Big Brother → Bigg Boss** and other shows whose formats have been adapted for different markets.

> A platform needs the appropriate rights to use and adapt an existing format.

## 24. Key Takeaways — Lecture 3

- OTT always involves costs somewhere in the ecosystem.
- Major OTT costs include **Technology, Content and Marketing**.
- Content must be regularly refreshed to reduce churn.
- Churn means a subscriber stops/cancels the subscription.
- Major churn factors include **content, price, ads, notifications, technology/app experience and event-based subscriptions**.
- Ad-supported OTT depends heavily on **audience volume**.
- OTT pricing must balance **price, volume, acquisition cost and revenue**.
- Content has different **formats, genres and classifications**.
- OTT platforms can **produce, licence, buy, livestream, commission or co-produce** content.
- Content rights can be **exclusive, non-exclusive, language-specific or geographically restricted**.
- OTT platforms can acquire existing content and adapt formats for local audiences.
- Business decisions depend on the platform's **audience, budget, strategy and data**.
