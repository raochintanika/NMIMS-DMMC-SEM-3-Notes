## 3. Five Digital Marketing Channels

### SEO — Search Engine Optimization

Improving website/content so it can appear organically in search-engine results.

**Key characteristics:** organic visibility, free/earned traffic, product discovery, long-term traffic, blogs/buying guides that compound over time.

### SEO → GEO

The lecture introduced **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)** as an evolution toward visibility in AI-generated/search experiences.

Example: “Best sunscreen for oily skin” shows search intent even if the person is not ready to buy.

### SEM — Search Engine Marketing

Using **paid search advertising** to gain visibility.

**SEO = earn visibility**

**SEM = pay for visibility**

Advantages: immediate visibility, measurable, useful for launches, quick traffic, search-intent targeting and PPC optimization.

### Content Marketing

Creating useful, entertaining or educational content that **pulls people toward the brand**.

Content can educate, entertain, build trust, answer questions, support purchase decisions, generate traffic, be shared, reduce uncertainty and potentially reduce returns.

**Content compounds.**

### Email Marketing

Sending **targeted, permission-based communication**.

Useful for personalization, retention, bringing customers back and abandoned-cart recovery.[^1]

Examples:

1. Welcome email
2. Offers
3. Brand story
4. Reminders
5. Updates
6. Transactions
7. FOMO
8. Personalized communication

**Email = relationship + retention + repeat purchase**

### Social Media Marketing

Useful for brand discovery, awareness, community, engagement, organic content, creators/influencers, paid advertising, product discovery and sales.

**Attention → Awareness → Interest → Community → Purchase**

## 4. How the Channels Work Together

| Channel | Main Role | Speed | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEO | Awareness → Consideration | Slow | Long-term organic traffic |
| SEM | Consideration → Purchase | Fast | Paid, measurable traffic |
| Content | Awareness → Trust | Slow | Education + authority |
| Email | Purchase → Retention | Fast | Relationship + loyalty |
| Social | Awareness → Community | Fast | Brand voice + visibility |

### Mental Model

**SEO + Content:** Earn attention

**SEM + Social:** Capture attention

**Email:** Keep the relationship alive

**Overall: Attract → Engage → Convert → Retain**

## 1. E-Commerce & Digital Marketing Ecosystem

### What Is E-Commerce & Digital Marketing?

The use of digital channels — search, social, content and email — to attract, convert and retain customers for a business that sells online.

**E-Commerce** is the business of selling online.

**Digital Marketing** is everything that gets the right person to that product — and back again.

### E-Commerce Ecosystem

Examples:

Fast delivery and increasing use of advertising as a revenue stream.

#### D2C & Social Commerce

Examples:

- Mamaearth

- Instagram / WhatsApp Shops

Brand-owned relationships, content and community-led commerce.

#### Value & Reseller Commerce

## 3. SEO — Search Engine Optimization

### SEO Checklist

- Right keywords and real search intent
- Fast-loading, mobile-friendly pages
- Clear titles and meta descriptions
- Quality backlinks from trusted sites
- Fresh, useful content

### Nykaa Case Study

Nykaa was discussed as an example of using:

- Long-form beauty guides
- Ingredient explainers
- “Best of” listicles

Example search intent:

**“Best sunscreen for oily skin India”**

The key learning was that SEO should answer the real question first rather than rely on keyword stuffing.

### SEO in 2026

Search is increasingly being influenced by AI-generated results.

The lecture introduced:

- **GEO — Generative Engine Optimization**
- **AEO — Answer Engine Optimization**

The objective is increasingly not only to rank in search results but also to become a useful source that AI/search experiences can cite.

## 4. SEM — Search Engine Marketing

### What It Is

Running paid ads on search engines and marketplaces so products can appear when users search for them.

Examples discussed:

- Google Ads
- Amazon
- Flipkart
- Myntra

### Why It Matters

- Instant visibility
- Highly measurable
- Useful for launches
- Useful for time-bound sales
- Targets existing search intent

**SEO = earn visibility**

**SEM = pay for visibility**

### SEM Case Study — boAt

The class discussed boAt's festive-sale strategy using sponsored ads and retail-media placements around high-intent searches during major sale periods.

### SEM Checklist

- Clear campaign goal
- Appropriate keyword targeting
- Negative keywords to reduce wasted spend[^2]
- Landing page matches the ad promise
- Monitor budget and bids

### Retail Media

The lecture highlighted the growing importance of retail media.

Brands can advertise directly on marketplaces and quick-commerce platforms where purchase intent is already high.

Examples:

- Amazon Ads
- Flipkart Ads
- Blinkit Ads

## 5. Content Marketing

### What It Is

Creating useful or entertaining content — videos, blogs, guides and reels — that pulls people toward the brand by helping or entertaining them first.

### Why It Matters

- Builds trust
- Highly shareable
- Educates buyers
- Can reduce returns and support queries
- Compounds over time

### Mamaearth Case Study

Mamaearth was discussed as an example of ingredient-education content across YouTube and Instagram.

The content answers real questions before directly pushing a product.

### Content Checklist

- Solve a real question first
- Use native formats for each platform
- Maintain a consistent posting cadence
- Repurpose one asset into multiple formats
- Track saves and shares, not only likes

### Content in 2026

The lecture discussed the shift toward:

- AI-assisted content creation
- User-generated content (UGC)
- Creator-economy investment
- Short-form video
- More human and imperfect-looking content

**Key idea:** Content that feels made by a person for people can perform strongly because it feels native to the platform.

## 6. Email Marketing

### What It Is

Sending targeted, permission-based emails directly to a customer's inbox.

### Why It Matters

- The brand owns the subscriber relationship
- Useful for retention
- Useful for abandoned-cart recovery
- Supports repeat purchases

### Email Lifecycle

1. **Welcome email** → sets the first impression
2. **Browse/cart abandonment** → nudges the sale
3. **Post-purchase** → builds loyalty and reviews
4. **Win-back** → re-engages lapsed buyers

### Myntra Case Study

The class discussed behaviour-triggered email flows, including cart-abandonment and post-purchase communication.

The key learning:

**Right trigger + Right message + Right moment**

### Email Checklist

- Clear consent at signup
- Welcome series
- One trigger per key event
- Concise subject lines
- Easy unsubscribe

### Email in 2026

The lecture discussed:

- AI-powered personalization
- Interactive email elements
- Behaviour-based messaging
- Data privacy and consent requirements

## 7. Social Media Marketing

### What It Is

Building brand presence and driving sales through platforms such as:

- Instagram
- YouTube
- X

Methods include:

- Organic content
- Influencer partnerships
- Paid social advertising

### Why It Matters

- Meets customers where attention already exists
- Builds brand voice and community
- Influencers can act as trusted recommendations
- Provides a fast feedback loop

### Zepto & Blinkit Case Study

Zepto and Blinkit were discussed for their witty, meme-fluent social presence.

They react quickly to trends and build a recognizable brand personality around quick commerce.

### Social Checklist

- One consistent brand voice
- React to culture quickly
- Mix organic content with paid boosts
- Track shares and saves
- Maintain a human presence behind the account

### Social Commerce

The lecture discussed the growing role of creators and social platforms in commerce.

**Creators → Content → Discovery → Trust → Purchase**

Creators are increasingly moving from being only advertising space toward becoming part of the selling experience.

## 8. Putting the Five Channels Together

| Channel | Funnel Stage | Speed | Cost | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SEO | Awareness → Consideration | Slow to build | Low, ongoing | Long-term organic traffic |
| SEM | Consideration → Purchase | Instant | Pay-per-click | Launches, sales events |
| Content Marketing | Awareness → Trust | Slow to build | Low–Medium | Education, brand trust |
| Email Marketing | Purchase → Retention | Fast | Very low | Cart recovery, loyalty |
| Social Media | Awareness → Community | Fast | Low–High | Brand voice, virality |

### Rule of Thumb

**SEO + Content → Earn attention**

**SEM + Social → Capture attention**

**Email → Keep the relationship alive**

**Integrated Marketing: Attract → Engage → Convert → Retain**
